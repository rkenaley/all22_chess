## 🌟 Why Simulation GIFs Defeat Still Cards

Static infographic cards (`.png`) are great for reading statistics, but American football is a game of **continuous motion, timing, and spatial geometry**. A still frame cannot show:
1. **Pre-Snap Motion & Tells**: The 4.21 or 4.31 speed receiver motioning across the formation to diagnose man vs. zone leverage.
2. **Backfield Mechanics**: The compact backward pitch ($t = 0.18\text{s}$, $-0.55\text{y}$) or direct shotgun snap while the second quarterback executes a weakside bootleg freeze.
3. **Pulling Linemen & Lead Convoys**: 4.85-speed Center Graham Barton and 4.94-speed Tanor Bortolini kicking out overhangs and climbing to linebackers in real time.
4. **Gated Lateral Physics**: Tyler Warren securing the ball in the zone void and waiting for Xavier Worthy's separation to reach $\ge 3.25\text{y}$ before pitching.
5. **Ball Flight & Downfield Acceleration**: Parabolic pass trajectories, receiver breaks, and violent contact at the tackle or end-zone pylon.

Every GIF runs at **20 frames per second (50ms per frame)** matching the underlying kinematic integration step ($dt = 0.05\text{s}$) in 1:1 real-time speed, with a **1.4-second hold on the final frame** to clearly review the play gain, first down, or touchdown before smoothly looping.

## 🛠️ CLI Generation Commands

To regenerate all GIFs or a specific offensive philosophy anytime:

```bash
# Generate all 16 GIFs across both Argon and Hydra
npm run generate:gifs

# Generate only Google Argon (8 plays)
npx tsx src/generate_play_gifs.ts ARGON

# Generate only Hydra Vanguard (8 plays)
npx tsx src/generate_play_gifs.ts HYDRA
```

All GIFs are stored in `docs/argon_gifs/` and `docs/hydra_gifs/` with file sizes between 1.8 MB and 3.5 MB, making them lightweight and compatible with Twitter/X's 15 MB limit, Discord's default upload ceiling, and Slack inline previews.
